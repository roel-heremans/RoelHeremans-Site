How I work

AI does the heavy lifting. I do the checking.

An AI coding assistant can write in an afternoon what used to take a team a few weeks. It can also be confidently wrong. My job is to make sure it isn't, before anything reaches your board or your customers.

Step 1

Scope

you + me

We pin down the business question, the decision it feeds, and what "correct" looks like. I get read access to your data and nothing more.

Step 2

Build

claude code, directed by me

Queries, models, pipelines and dashboards are written quickly with Claude Code, under my design and in your stack.

Step 3

Verify

me, by hand

I reconcile every headline number against the source, test edge cases and challenge results that look too good. Anything that fails goes back to step 2.

Step 4

Deliver

you own it

You get readable code, a refreshable dashboard or report, and plain-English notes on definitions, assumptions and limits.

What "hand-tested" means

  • Totals are reconciled against the raw source system, not against the AI's own output.
  • Key figures are re-derived a second, independent way.
  • Individual records are traced end to end through the pipeline.
  • Effects are checked for confounders, such as a product launch or price change, before they are called a result.
  • Every definition and assumption is written down, so your team can challenge it.

CurrentHealthcare · Retail pharmacy

Best Way Healthcare

Data scientist for the commercial and clinical analytics of a UK pharmacy group's weight-management service, online and in store.

I built and maintain an analytics dashboard that refreshes itself several times every business day. It covers the full patient journey: acquisition funnel, retention and churn, pricing and margins, clinical outcomes, geography and promotions.

Hand-checking pays off here. A rise in "churn" turned out to be largely patients who had reached their weight goal. An apparent uplift from a promotion turned out to be the effect of a new product launch. Both findings changed what the business decided to do next.

Other work includes a price response model, campaign and geo-experiment reviews, a transformer-based classifier for pharmacist notes, and planning a patient 360 data model in Microsoft Fabric. All of it uses pseudonymised data and is handled with GDPR in mind.

Weather · Consumer appsvia Proxify

Impala Studios: severe-weather triggers with GraphCast

Impala Studios publishes NOAA Weather Radar, a US weather app. Their marketing team found out about severe weather by reading the news, which was often too late to launch a campaign.

I built a pipeline around Google DeepMind's GraphCast (and GenCast), an AI weather model. It pulls ERA5 data from Copernicus and runs daily forecasts on RunPod GPUs and a Mac Studio.

On top of the forecasts I wrote physics-based detectors for hurricanes, severe precipitation, and storm cells and supercells. When an event crosses its thresholds, a trigger sends a validated JSON alert to Impala's alerts API and to Slack. It includes event type, intensity, location and time window, so marketing can act before the event reaches mainstream media.

Forecasts are evaluated against observed data, and the triggers have dry-run mode, retries and logging so they can be trusted in production.
